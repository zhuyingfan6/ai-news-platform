### Supply Chain News Brief

This week’s headlines highlight a major push toward automation and infrastructure investment by retail giants, alongside significant shifts in logistics networks driven by tariffs and urban efficiency pilots. Key developments include Amazon’s massive European robotics investment, Walmart’s first international membership expansion, and a critical report showing most companies are unprepared for future disruptions.

**Key News Points by Topic:**

- **Retail & E-commerce Expansion:**
    - **Walmart+** launched in Canada, marking its first expansion outside the U.S. with a bundled membership including delivery and Crave streaming.
    - **Target** invested $367 million in a new food distribution center in Colorado.
    - **Amazon** announced an $11.6 billion investment in robotics across Europe to accelerate delivery speeds.

- **Warehouse & Logistics Automation:**
    - **Amazon** also reached a milestone of 50,000 electric delivery vans deployed worldwide.
    - **UPS** announced a $50 million investment to expand its air freight network in Mexico.
    - New research suggests warehouse robots need to adopt "swarm" intelligence, mimicking ant behavior for better efficiency.
    - **Honeywell** sold its warehouse technology unit to Brady for $1.4 billion.

- **Infrastructure & Urban Logistics:**
    - A pilot program in **NYC** using microhubs successfully eliminated 3,000 truck trips in just one year.
    - **Texas ports** reported $107 billion in imports related to data center construction.
    - **USA Rare Earth** selected Blacksburg, South Carolina for a new $1.2 billion magnet manufacturing plant.

- **Shipping & Freight Trends:**
    - A new report reveals that nearly one-third of global container shipments are now traveling empty.
    - Air freight volumes jumped 12% as companies shift shipping routes in response to tariffs.
    - The **USPS** is cutting spending as it projects its cash reserves will run out by 2027.

- **Risk & Technology:**
    - A new report finds that only **7% of companies** actively model future supply chain disruptions.
    - **Coupa** and **MIT** launched a new AI-powered Business Spend Index to track economic activity.
    - **DHL** predicts that AI agents could be shopping for consumers autonomously by 2031.
